# Visual assets

## Toronto skyline

The site background uses `public/images/toronto-paper-skyline.webp` for the CN Tower and detailed building cutouts, a paper illustration created with the built-in image generation tool using Osama Saeed's Toronto waterfront photograph as its architectural reference. The source was the homepage's previous hero photograph: https://unsplash.com/photos/cn-tower-grayscale-photography-trq3hS53NYU. The photographer is credited over the artwork in the hero.

Generation prompt: “Transform this photograph into a polished 2D layered cut-paper and screen-print illustration. Preserve the actual Toronto skyline's composition and distinctive building geometry closely: CN Tower exactly centered with its narrow antenna, broad circular observation deck and long shaft; the tall bright-crowned office tower left of center; the dense stepped condos on the left; the two slender condo towers to the right; waterfront tree line and narrow lake strip at bottom. Keep the proportions, roof heights, horizontal spacing, and viewpoint from the photograph. Crisp architectural details and many small facade lines, with 3 to 5 overlapping paper color layers, subtle fibrous paper texture and offset cut edges. Warm cream cloudy sky, deep indigo and slate-blue buildings, muted sand waterfront, pale blue water. Editorial printmaking quality, sophisticated, recognizably the exact photographed city. No generic skyline, no added buildings, no stylized fantasy architecture, no text, no border, no watermark.”

The full-viewport scene takes its layered-paper direction from [Foxglove Hollow](https://miaai-lab.github.io/Claude-Opus-5.5-100-HTML-Files/018-paper-cut-diorama.html). It stays behind the portfolio content while scrolling. `app/modules/portfolio-page/toronto-skyline-art.tsx` draws separate sky, city, tree, shore, and water sheets. The detailed buildings, CN Tower, trees, and shore share one movement offset, so facades and waterfront structures do not split at cutout boundaries; the water and sky retain independent motion. Day and Night use the same cutout boundaries. A color-key filter removes the day illustration's cream sky within its building cutouts. The CN Tower cutout ends where the shaft meets the roofs, leaving no source building facade attached below it. Fine procedural paper grain, a hand-cut mat, pale upper edges, and progressively deeper sheet shadows add tactile depth. The art also overlays water glints, sun/moon glow, rain, and snow. `portfolio-page.module.css` adjusts the artwork's light and color for Toronto's time of day.

Live mode follows Toronto local time, while Day and Night buttons let visitors preview either version. Night uses its own indigo paper palette, stars, moon glow, illuminated windows and tower, and reflected light on the water. Weather conditions come from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs); the scene stays clear when the API is unavailable. Current cloud cover controls the opacity of two drifting paper cloud sheets behind the skyline, and precipitation controls rain at two depths with ripples on the lake. Snow also falls in two depth layers, using seamless vertical repeats and independent side-to-side sway. Wind direction and speed influence the weather motion. The weather selector previews these conditions independently of Day and Night. The client refreshes time every minute and weather every 30 minutes. Ambient animation respects reduced-motion preferences.

The floating Depth lab is a development-only tuning jig for the layered scene and the portfolio's visual experiments. It switches among five work entrances and six title materials. Its parallax slider scales the sky, land, and water cursor response; layer spacing changes the distance between the land and water sheets; shadow reach changes the SVG cutout shadows; and cut edge changes the pale rim on paper silhouettes. Save settings persists these choices in local storage, while Reset restores the defaults. Production uses the default depth settings, deckled title label, and Lift the City work treatment without rendering the jig.

The night version uses `public/images/toronto-paper-skyline-night.webp`, a purpose-made nighttime edit of the day illustration. It preserves the skyline's composition and adds illuminated windows, red and blue CN Tower lighting, dark paper clouds, and waterfront reflections. [Felix55kerstan's shaft and observation ring photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cn_tower_night_view.jpg) and [TheWxResearcher's full tower photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CN_Tower_at_night_2024-08-23.jpg) informed the tower lighting. `public/images/toronto-paper-day-sky.webp` and `public/images/toronto-paper-night-sky.webp` are matching sky-only backplates. The city illustration appears only inside the moving building and tower cutouts; there is no full skyline copy behind them. Trees, shore, and water remain separate foreground sheets. Both versions follow the hand-traced CN Tower silhouette.

## Title label previews

The hero's title label has six selectable CSS treatments in two families. Paper comes as deckled handmade stock, a blue ruled letter with a folded corner, or grainy newsprint with offset ink. Tickets come as the original ochre transit ticket, a dark punch pass with stub and holes, or a narrow thermal fare receipt with a torn edge and barcode. Each reuses the procedural `public/images/toronto-paper-grain.svg` texture, with material-specific edges, borders, shadows, and lettering. The development-only preview buttons live inside the Depth lab and update the label in place on Day or Night without changing the skyline.

## Work entrance experiments

The development jig switches among five complete treatments for the hero invitation and project section: Lift the City uses stacked sheets that open to reveal case studies; Streetcar Route lays out projects as stops along a line; Field Guide turns them into annotated foldout chapters; Paper Theatre presents them as navigable acts on a stage; and Postcards gives each project an illustrated front and written back. Each treatment keeps the complete project content and a direct link from the hero to the work section. The experience and contact sections pick up the selected treatment's materials and colors. The jig links back to the hero and forward to the work section for comparison.
