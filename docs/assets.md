# Visual assets

## Toronto skyline

The site background uses `public/images/toronto-paper-skyline.webp` for the CN Tower and detailed building cutouts, a paper illustration created with the built-in image generation tool using Osama Saeed's Toronto waterfront photograph as its architectural reference. The source was the homepage's previous hero photograph: https://unsplash.com/photos/cn-tower-grayscale-photography-trq3hS53NYU. The photographer is credited over the artwork in the hero.

Generation prompt: “Transform this photograph into a polished 2D layered cut-paper and screen-print illustration. Preserve the actual Toronto skyline's composition and distinctive building geometry closely: CN Tower exactly centered with its narrow antenna, broad circular observation deck and long shaft; the tall bright-crowned office tower left of center; the dense stepped condos on the left; the two slender condo towers to the right; waterfront tree line and narrow lake strip at bottom. Keep the proportions, roof heights, horizontal spacing, and viewpoint from the photograph. Crisp architectural details and many small facade lines, with 3 to 5 overlapping paper color layers, subtle fibrous paper texture and offset cut edges. Warm cream cloudy sky, deep indigo and slate-blue buildings, muted sand waterfront, pale blue water. Editorial printmaking quality, sophisticated, recognizably the exact photographed city. No generic skyline, no added buildings, no stylized fantasy architecture, no text, no border, no watermark.”

The full-viewport scene takes its layered-paper direction from [Foxglove Hollow](https://miaai-lab.github.io/Claude-Opus-5.5-100-HTML-Files/018-paper-cut-diorama.html). It stays behind the portfolio content while scrolling. `app/modules/toronto-scene/toronto-skyline-art.tsx` draws separate sky, city, tree, shore, and water sheets. The detailed buildings, CN Tower, trees, and shore share one movement offset, so facades and waterfront structures do not split at cutout boundaries; the water and sky retain independent motion. Day and Night use the same cutout boundaries. A color-key filter removes the day illustration's cream sky within its building cutouts. The CN Tower cutout ends where the shaft meets the roofs, leaving no source building facade attached below it. Fine procedural paper grain, a hand-cut mat, pale upper edges, and progressively deeper sheet shadows add tactile depth. The art also overlays water glints, sun/moon glow, rain, and snow. `toronto-scene.module.css` adjusts the artwork's light and color for Toronto's time of day.

Live mode follows Toronto local time, while Dawn, Day, Dusk and Night buttons let visitors preview each version. Night uses its own indigo paper palette, stars, moon glow, illuminated windows and tower, and reflected light on the water. Weather conditions come from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs); the scene stays clear when the API is unavailable. Current cloud cover controls the opacity of two drifting paper cloud sheets behind the skyline, and precipitation controls rain at two depths with ripples on the lake. Snow also falls in two depth layers, using seamless vertical repeats and independent side-to-side sway. Wind direction and speed influence the weather motion. The weather selector previews these conditions independently of Day and Night. The client refreshes time every minute and weather every 30 minutes. `SceneProvider` (`app/modules/toronto-scene/scene-provider.tsx`) owns this state, including the live temperature, so page layouts can read and describe the current scene through `useScene()` and `describeScene()`. Ambient animation respects reduced-motion preferences.

The floating Depth lab is a development-only tuning jig for the layered scene. Its parallax slider scales the sky, land, and water cursor response; layer spacing changes the distance between the land and water sheets; shadow reach changes the SVG cutout shadows; and cut edge changes the pale rim on paper silhouettes. Save settings persists the current values in local storage, while Reset restores the default values and clears the saved values. Production uses the default depth settings and does not render the lab.

The night version uses `public/images/toronto-paper-skyline-night.webp`, a purpose-made nighttime edit of the day illustration. It preserves the skyline's composition and adds illuminated windows, red and blue CN Tower lighting, dark paper clouds, and waterfront reflections. [Felix55kerstan's shaft and observation ring photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cn_tower_night_view.jpg) and [TheWxResearcher's full tower photo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CN_Tower_at_night_2024-08-23.jpg) informed the tower lighting. `public/images/toronto-paper-day-sky.webp` and `public/images/toronto-paper-night-sky.webp` are matching sky-only backplates. The city illustration appears only inside the moving building and tower cutouts; there is no full skyline copy behind them. Trees, shore, and water remain separate foreground sheets. Both versions follow the hand-traced CN Tower silhouette.

## Pinned to the sky

The page content is part of the diorama (`app/modules/pinned-sky/`). Paper cards hang in the sky at three depths, fastened with push pins, tape or thread: a name card, a card per case study, a roles card and a profiles tag. On wide screens they surround the CN Tower and follow the cursor at different rates; below 76rem they hang in a staggered column with sky showing between them. Opening a project card unfolds it into a sheet (a native `<dialog>`) and records it in the URL hash, so `#<case study id>` opens that project directly. Cards sway with the live wind, weather and time of day, and settle while hovered or focused. The skyline controls sit behind the "Change the sky" button.

## Motion lab

Backdrop motion and weather are pluggable passes (`app/modules/toronto-scene/motion/`). A pass (`ScenePass` in `motion-types.ts`) can add a class to the scene root and restyle any `data-scene-layer` element, run a `Driver` for pointer, scroll or timer input, draw SVG into the `Sky`, `Mid` or `Front` layers in the artwork's 1536×1024 space, draw HTML (such as a canvas) over the whole scene through `Screen`, and switch off built-in effects it redraws through `replaces`. Production uses the original passes: cursor parallax and the built-in clouds, rain and snow. The original parallax is `useCursorParallax` in `original-passes.tsx`.

The development-only Motion lab (bottom left) lists every pass in `motion-registry.ts`, loading each on demand, so production never bundles them. It keeps the choice in `?backdrop=` and `?weather-fx=`, previews any lighting and weather, and "Hide page" hides the layout to show the scene alone. `motion.test.tsx` renders every pass in six lighting and weather combinations.
